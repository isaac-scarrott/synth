# 2026-07-29

## The active-pane focus strip is gone

A split's active pane carried a 2px bar across its whole top edge in the mark colour. It's gone.

The bar was the loudest thing in the window and it was spending that weight on the least surprising
fact on screen — which pane you just clicked into. A strip that wide reads as a heading for the pane
under it, so every split gained a title rule that titled nothing. In tabs mode it was worse: the
strip ran the full width of a pane's content directly beneath a tab strip whose active tab already
carries the same 2px bar in the same colour, two rules of the same weight stacked a few pixels
apart, the wider one belonging to the thing that mattered less.

Which pane is active was never only the bar's job. The sidebar echo band already wears the copper
open-tile on the active member, and in tabs mode the active tab wears the mark bar — both of them
naming the session, which is what you actually look for. The keyboard state that `activePane` drives
(⌘⌥ arrows, ⌘1…9, resize) is unchanged; only its full-width advertisement is.

`--focus` stays — the active-tab bar and the toast countdown are its remaining users, and the
countdown's comment now inherits its grammar from the tab bar rather than from the pane bar that no
longer exists.

## A gate run happens on your machine without happening to you

Verifying Synth means running Synth: the agent gates drive a real build, and so does every agent that
ports a change and has to prove it works. All of that happens on the machine its owner is working on,
and until now it happened *at* them. A driven build was an ordinary Synth — `.regular` activation
policy and `NSApp.activate(ignoringOtherApps:)` at launch — so each of the nineteen suites, several of
which launch two or three instances, took the keyboard out of whatever sentence was being typed, put a
window in front of whatever was being read, hid the cursor on every synthetic keystroke, and fired
real Notification Center banners with sound. A suite is minutes long. The work was already headless in
every way that mattered — the control socket drives it, the screenshot is rendered in-process, no
CGEvent ever leaves the app — the app had simply never been told that nobody is watching.

`SYNTH_AUTOMATION=1` now means invisible as well as drivable (`Automation.swift`). The app launches
`.accessory` and never activates: no Dock icon, no ⌘Tab slot, no focus taken from anyone. Its windows
— the app's own and the ⌘K panel, which is a window in its own right and would otherwise flash a
palette over the desktop — are made unseeable rather than moved: `alphaValue = 0`, deaf to the
pointer, at desktop level, out of Mission Control and ⌘`, but still ordered in at full size, because
`automation.screenshot` renders a window's content view and one ordered out or shrunk would take the
visual evidence path with it. Moving them away was the first attempt and doesn't hold: AppKit
constrains a titled window's frame back onto a screen, which on a second display parks a whole legible
Synth in the open. Transparency has no such argument with the window server, and it costs nothing —
the terminal contrast gate still reads ghostty's Metal output through `cacheDisplay`, and ⌘K driven
over the socket still screenshots as a palette, both from windows that draw to nobody.

Two smaller thefts went with it. Typing hides the mouse pointer until it next moves, which is a
system-wide setting a *synthetic* keystroke has no business touching, so a driven build doesn't. And
the window frame is saved in the bundle's own defaults — the domain the developer's next dev launch
reads — so a driven window's autosave is cut before anything else is done to it.

Notification Center posts are now recorded instead of delivered. A gate that pins the unfocused route
is asserting the routing rule, not asking for a banner on someone's desktop; `automation.notifs`
returns the posts the run would have made, which makes that branch assertable for the first time — the
banner never was. t3 uses it to prove a background needs-input reaches Notification Center naming its
row, and t11 to prove the update card is the one attention card that never does.

The porting skill's own instructions were half the problem and are rewritten to match: `TESTING.md`
told agents to raise toasts by `osascript … frontmost` on their instance (that is, to take the user's
focus deliberately) and offered a `screencapture -l<WINID>` fallback that needed the window on the
active Space. Both are gone — `automation.notifRoute` / `notifFocus` say what focus is instead of
taking it, and the in-process shot is the only capture path. `drive.swift` and `findwin.swift` go with
them; `automation.key` posts keystrokes inside the app, addressed to its own window, so they land
without the app ever being frontmost. `capture.sh` builds, launches driven and screenshots over the
socket.

Running the gates against it turned up one thing of its own: t12 read the ⌘K palette a fixed 0.4s
after posting the keystroke, which is a race — a posted key is dispatched a runloop turn later and
opening the palette builds a window — and it lost that race once under load. It now waits for the
palette the way t11 already did.

What none of this changes: every verb still runs the exact product path the UI runs. The run is
quieter, not thinner.
