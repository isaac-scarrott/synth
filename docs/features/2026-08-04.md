# 2026-08-04

## The window is translucent, terminal included

Synth's window now sits *on* the desktop rather than in front of it: the window server blurs the
wallpaper behind it, and every surface above that is a plain alpha fill over the one blurred sample.
The reference point was a Ghostty setup at `background-opacity = 0.75` / `background-blur = 20`,
deliberately dialled back — the desktop is a wash under the app, never a subject.

**The terminal participates.** The two cheaper scopes — chrome only, or everything-but-terminal —
were rejected, and it is worth writing down why, because the terminal is the risky part and the cheap
versions are much less work. A translucent shell wrapped around an opaque black rectangle does not
read as a translucent app; it reads as a bug, because the terminal is the largest single surface in a
working pane and the eye goes straight to the one thing that did not move. Half the effect costs
nearly all of the risk to legibility and buys none of the impression.

### The blur is not an NSVisualEffectView, and that decided everything else

This started with `.underWindowBackground` — the material literally named for this position in the
stack — and it was wrong. Every AppKit material **tints as well as blurs**, and that tint multiplies
with the surface alpha above it exactly the way a second coat would. Behind the alphas the design file
had been tuned to, it left roughly 4% of the wallpaper showing and dragged light mode's near-white
down to a flat grey. Translucent on paper, invisible in the room.

So Synth asks the window server directly: `CGSSetWindowBackgroundBlurRadius`, radius 60, which adds no
tint at all. That is what CSS `backdrop-filter: blur()` does — which is why `working.html` could be
trusted to tune against in the first place — and what Ghostty does, which is where the look came from.

It is **private API**, and it is treated as a progressive enhancement rather than a dependency: the
symbols are resolved once through `dlsym`, and if they are missing `WindowBlur.isAvailable` is false,
the window stays opaque and `Theme.windowCoat` resolves to a solid colour. A macOS that drops the call
makes Synth look the way it did before any of this existed. The failure mode to avoid is not "no blur"
— it is a translucent shell over a *sharp* desktop, which is unreadable, and that is the one outcome
the fallback rules out.

### One coat, and tints on it

The blur only samples what is behind the *window*, so there is exactly one of it, at the root. That
forces the rule the rest follows: there is also exactly one **translucent coat**
(`Theme.windowCoat`), and anything that needs to differ from it is a tint *on* it, never a second
coat.

Two coats compound. A sidebar at 0.84 over a pane coat at 0.86 leaves 2% of the desktop where they
overlap and 14% where they don't, so the wallpaper is visibly more present in the pane than in the
sidebar. Worse, the sidebar's 20pt rounded corners are cut *out* of its fill, so the notch behind them
showed a hole straight through to the wallpaper with no surface underneath.

So the sidebar became `Theme.sidebarStep` — `mono(0.04, 0.025)`, a tint of the ink rather than a
colour. A fixed colour breathes with the wallpaper: the step between sidebar and pane opens and closes
as the coat rides up and down under it. A multiplicative tint holds its ratio, measuring ~9.5 levels
across the whole photo, sky to slate. `Theme.sidebar` keeps its opaque hex; all that is left for it is
the rails in ⌘? and the changelog, which sit on a dialog rather than on the coat.

### Dark carries 0.11 more opacity than light, and that gap is the finding

Not a rounding choice. The same amount of wallpaper light lands completely differently depending on
what it lands on: against light's 250 it is a ~3% change and nearly invisible; against dark's 25 it is
a ~28% *lift* and the surface goes milky. A near-equal pair read as too transparent in dark and too
flat in light **at the same time**, which is what made the asymmetry obvious. 0.86 light, 0.97 dark.

Radius and coat are separate dials and it is worth knowing which to reach for: radius decides how
*recognisable* the desktop is, and costs nothing; the coat decides how much light gets through at all,
and costs legibility. Reach for radius first. Radius 20 — Ghostty's own setting — was far too sharp
for a whole shell, and a recognisable photo reads as "too transparent" at the same alpha that a soft
wash reads as right.

### It cost one grey, and there was no alternative

`--ink-meta` had to darken 16 levels in light, #6B6E76 → #5B5E66. The tier cleared 4.5:1 with no
margin at all against the opaque sidebar (4.52:1), so *any* coat put it under — 3.56:1 over the
wallpaper's dark end. The arithmetic says raising the coat cannot fix it: the sidebar only clears 4.5
again at a fully opaque 1.00, so there is no light coat value that keeps a visible effect and the old
ramp. Moving the ink was the only move that kept both, and it lifts the tier to 6.26:1 on the opaque
dialogs and rails that also use it. Dark needed nothing — it holds 5.04:1 at the 0.97 coat.

`--text-muted` and `--text-faint` were deliberately below 4.5 already, as secondary tiers, and
degrade proportionally rather than crossing a line they were holding.

### The terminal, and the seam that gave it away

Ghostty paints the whole card now — cells *and* the `window-padding` inset that frames them — at
`background-opacity` 0.55 light / 0.58 dark. Low only because the card nests inside the coat and the
two multiply: at the chrome's own alpha the terminal would show a fraction of a percent and be opaque
in all but name. As it is, about 6% of the desktop reaches the cells in light and barely 1% in dark,
which is what keeps the photo's texture out of the text.

`TermSurface` used to paint `Theme.tuiBg` behind the ghostty view as well, and that was a genuine bug
translucency exposed: the padding band got one coat and the cells got two, so the terminal read as
*more solid than its own border*. Ghostty's inset absorbed the SwiftUI padding (`window-padding` 23/19)
and `Theme.tuiBg` is deleted — there is one painter and one alpha, and no pair of numbers to keep in
step by hand. That config is global, so the scratch terminal dropped its own 14/11 inset to match;
1–2pt off its old geometry, which is not worth a second per-surface config to reclaim.

### Two more things that had quietly depended on an opaque surface

A pattern worth naming, because all three bugs this change surfaced were the same shape: something
relying on an opaque fill to occlude what was behind it.

**The terminal card's drop shadow.** SwiftUI's `.shadow` blurs the view's *alpha*, so the moment the
card's fill stopped being opaque, its own shadow showed through it — a uniform grey wash across the
cells, 7 levels deep, measured. CSS never had the problem: an outer `box-shadow` is clipped to outside
the border-box. The halo is now cast from an opaque stand-in with the card's footprint subtracted by an
even-odd path (`OutsideRoundedRect`). A `.destinationOut` blend was tried first and punched through the
window's coat as well, dropping the card onto the bare desktop — a path subtraction composites nothing
and guesses nothing. Worth keeping rather than dropping the shadow, because on light the translucent
card composites to within a shade of the pane, so the halo and the hairline are the only things left
saying "card".

**The tabs-mode first-tab bleed.** It was a plain 20pt rectangle slid left under the sidebar, correct
only because an opaque sidebar hid everything but the corner wedge. Against a 4% tint the whole
rectangle showed through as a bright block with a hard square edge. It is now the wedge itself
(`SidebarCornerWedge`), so the fill follows the sidebar's curve and stops where the curve does — height
equal to the radius, because below the arc the sidebar's edge runs straight and there is no gap to fill.

### Prototyping it

`working.html` grew a fixed backdrop layer and a dev-only switcher — `photo` / `night` / `gradient` —
because a browser cannot sample the desktop and translucency judged against nothing is not judged at
all. The photo is the actual wallpaper this was tuned against, vendored beside the fonts for the same
reason they are (a design file that silently falls back is one you can review and be wrong about):
mid-tone sky over high-frequency slate, which stresses light and dark chrome at once. `night` is that
same image graded down rather than a second binary. None of it ports to the app.

The prototype earned its keep and also showed its limit. It caught the compounding rule, the notch and
the sidebar step; it could not predict the material's tint, and its alphas did not survive contact with
a real desktop at native resolution — dark moved 0.88 → 0.97. A CSS blur over a downscaled JPEG is a
good model of an untinted blur and a bad model of how much of it you can afford.

*Rejected:* chrome-only and everything-but-terminal scopes (above); per-pane `backdrop-filter` in the
design file, which browsers compound and no AppKit route can reproduce, so the prototype would have
promised a look the port could not deliver; `state = .followsWindowActiveState` on the material, back
when there was a material — AppKit's default drops the effect whenever the window loses focus, and on a
shell that is translucent by design that reads as the window breaking.
## Tabs-mode branches carry their session facts without growing a third tree level

Tabs mode removes session rows from the sidebar because those sessions already live in the tab
strip. That made the branch the deepest visible row, but the old compact branch shell still spent
space on a disclosure chevron and hid the session count in a tooltip. The branch now uses that space
as a 46px two-line summary: its name first, then `N sessions · activity`.

The right edge is a fixed pair of 16px slots: pull-request state, then the branch's highest-priority
session state. Empty slots remain reserved, so names and status never jump between branches. Live,
error, needs-input, unread, pending, and PR states keep their established glyphs and colours; idle
activity moves into the facts line. Hover actions cover the whole rail rather than leaving a status
glyph behind.

This is tabs-mode presentation only. Classic sidebar mode keeps its 30px disclosure row, middle
truncation, inline PR identity, and activity rail unchanged. Rename preserves the two-line tabs-mode
geometry, and the status-only rail has explicit VoiceOver labels because it is the sole sidebar
signal once session rows are absent.

The HTML source and native SwiftUI app ship the same layout. Gate: `swift build`, an independent
code-to-spec audit, tabs-on and tabs-off screenshots from isolated running app instances, and the
`working.html` / `big-picture-design.html` subset diff.

## The pre-notarization Gatekeeper check asserts *why* it was rejected

`release.sh` assesses the freshly signed app with `spctl` before sending it to Apple. That stage's
only passing answer is `rejected` / `source=Unnotarized Developer ID` — Gatekeeper is right, the
ticket does not exist yet. The check was there to catch the *other* rejections, the ones that mean
the build is broken: unsigned nested code, a missing hardened runtime. But those are also a
`rejected`, and the line ended in `|| true`, so every outcome printed the same word and continued.
A guard that cannot fail is decoration, and the one reader it had was left to either panic at
`rejected` or learn to skim past it — which is how the real failure would have gone by.

It now captures the verdict, prints it, and requires the reason to be `source=Unnotarized Developer
ID`, dying on any other rejection. The stage also labels itself in the log, so the expected
`rejected` no longer needs a source comment to interpret it. The post-stapling assess is unchanged:
that one must be `accepted`, and `pipefail` already made it fatal.

Recorded alongside it in the release skill: `release.sh` must not be edited while it runs. Bash
reads a script incrementally, so an in-place rewrite resumes execution at a byte offset that now
falls mid-line. A release is ~20 minutes mostly spent waiting on Apple, which is precisely when
editing it feels free.
