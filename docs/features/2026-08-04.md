# 2026-08-04

## The window is translucent, terminal included

Synth's window now sits *on* the desktop rather than in front of it: the window server blurs the
wallpaper behind it, and every surface above that is a plain alpha fill over the one blurred sample.
The reference point was a Ghostty setup at `background-opacity = 0.75` / `background-blur = 20`,
deliberately dialled back — the desktop is a wash under the app, never a subject.

**The terminal participates.** The two cheaper scopes — chrome only, or everything-but-terminal —
were rejected, and it is worth writing down why, because the terminal is the risky part and the cheap
versions are much less work. A translucent shell wrapped around an opaque black rectangle does not
read as a translucent app; it reads as a bug, because the terminal is the largest single surface in a
working pane and the eye goes straight to the one thing that did not move. Half the effect costs
nearly all of the risk to legibility and buys none of the impression.

### The blur is not an NSVisualEffectView, and that decided everything else

This started with `.underWindowBackground` — the material literally named for this position in the
stack — and it was wrong. Every AppKit material **tints as well as blurs**, and that tint multiplies
with the surface alpha above it exactly the way a second coat would. Behind the alphas the design file
had been tuned to, it left roughly 4% of the wallpaper showing and dragged light mode's near-white
down to a flat grey. Translucent on paper, invisible in the room.

So Synth asks the window server directly: `CGSSetWindowBackgroundBlurRadius`, radius 60, which adds no
tint at all. That is what CSS `backdrop-filter: blur()` does — which is why `working.html` could be
trusted to tune against in the first place — and what Ghostty does, which is where the look came from.

It is **private API**, and it is treated as a progressive enhancement rather than a dependency: the
symbols are resolved once through `dlsym`, and if they are missing `WindowBlur.isAvailable` is false,
the window stays opaque and `Theme.windowCoat` resolves to a solid colour. A macOS that drops the call
makes Synth look the way it did before any of this existed. The failure mode to avoid is not "no blur"
— it is a translucent shell over a *sharp* desktop, which is unreadable, and that is the one outcome
the fallback rules out.

### One coat, and tints on it

The blur only samples what is behind the *window*, so there is exactly one of it, at the root. That
forces the rule the rest follows: there is also exactly one **translucent coat**
(`Theme.windowCoat`), and anything that needs to differ from it is a tint *on* it, never a second
coat.

Two coats compound. A sidebar at 0.84 over a pane coat at 0.86 leaves 2% of the desktop where they
overlap and 14% where they don't, so the wallpaper is visibly more present in the pane than in the
sidebar. Worse, the sidebar's 20pt rounded corners are cut *out* of its fill, so the notch behind them
showed a hole straight through to the wallpaper with no surface underneath.

So the sidebar became `Theme.sidebarStep` — `mono(0.04, 0.025)`, a tint of the ink rather than a
colour. A fixed colour breathes with the wallpaper: the step between sidebar and pane opens and closes
as the coat rides up and down under it. A multiplicative tint holds its ratio, measuring ~9.5 levels
across the whole photo, sky to slate. `Theme.sidebar` keeps its opaque hex; all that is left for it is
the rails in ⌘? and the changelog, which sit on a dialog rather than on the coat.

### Dark carries 0.11 more opacity than light, and that gap is the finding

Not a rounding choice. The same amount of wallpaper light lands completely differently depending on
what it lands on: against light's 250 it is a ~3% change and nearly invisible; against dark's 25 it is
a ~28% *lift* and the surface goes milky. A near-equal pair read as too transparent in dark and too
flat in light **at the same time**, which is what made the asymmetry obvious. 0.86 light, 0.97 dark.

Radius and coat are separate dials and it is worth knowing which to reach for: radius decides how
*recognisable* the desktop is, and costs nothing; the coat decides how much light gets through at all,
and costs legibility. Reach for radius first. Radius 20 — Ghostty's own setting — was far too sharp
for a whole shell, and a recognisable photo reads as "too transparent" at the same alpha that a soft
wash reads as right.

### It cost one grey, and there was no alternative

`--ink-meta` had to darken 16 levels in light, #6B6E76 → #5B5E66. The tier cleared 4.5:1 with no
margin at all against the opaque sidebar (4.52:1), so *any* coat put it under — 3.56:1 over the
wallpaper's dark end. The arithmetic says raising the coat cannot fix it: the sidebar only clears 4.5
again at a fully opaque 1.00, so there is no light coat value that keeps a visible effect and the old
ramp. Moving the ink was the only move that kept both, and it lifts the tier to 6.26:1 on the opaque
dialogs and rails that also use it. Dark needed nothing — it holds 5.04:1 at the 0.97 coat.

`--text-muted` and `--text-faint` were deliberately below 4.5 already, as secondary tiers, and
degrade proportionally rather than crossing a line they were holding.

### The terminal, and the seam that gave it away

Ghostty paints the whole card now — cells *and* the `window-padding` inset that frames them — at
`background-opacity` 0.55 light / 0.58 dark. Low only because the card nests inside the coat and the
two multiply: at the chrome's own alpha the terminal would show a fraction of a percent and be opaque
in all but name. As it is, about 6% of the desktop reaches the cells in light and barely 1% in dark,
which is what keeps the photo's texture out of the text.

`TermSurface` used to paint `Theme.tuiBg` behind the ghostty view as well, and that was a genuine bug
translucency exposed: the padding band got one coat and the cells got two, so the terminal read as
*more solid than its own border*. Ghostty's inset absorbed the SwiftUI padding (`window-padding` 23/19)
and `Theme.tuiBg` is deleted — there is one painter and one alpha, and no pair of numbers to keep in
step by hand. That config is global, so the scratch terminal dropped its own 14/11 inset to match;
1–2pt off its old geometry, which is not worth a second per-surface config to reclaim.

### Two more things that had quietly depended on an opaque surface

A pattern worth naming, because all three bugs this change surfaced were the same shape: something
relying on an opaque fill to occlude what was behind it.

**The terminal card's drop shadow.** SwiftUI's `.shadow` blurs the view's *alpha*, so the moment the
card's fill stopped being opaque, its own shadow showed through it — a uniform grey wash across the
cells, 7 levels deep, measured. CSS never had the problem: an outer `box-shadow` is clipped to outside
the border-box. The halo is now cast from an opaque stand-in with the card's footprint subtracted by an
even-odd path (`OutsideRoundedRect`). A `.destinationOut` blend was tried first and punched through the
window's coat as well, dropping the card onto the bare desktop — a path subtraction composites nothing
and guesses nothing. Worth keeping rather than dropping the shadow, because on light the translucent
card composites to within a shade of the pane, so the halo and the hairline are the only things left
saying "card".

**The tabs-mode first-tab bleed.** It was a plain 20pt rectangle slid left under the sidebar, correct
only because an opaque sidebar hid everything but the corner wedge. Against a 4% tint the whole
rectangle showed through as a bright block with a hard square edge. It is now the wedge itself
(`SidebarCornerWedge`), so the fill follows the sidebar's curve and stops where the curve does — height
equal to the radius, because below the arc the sidebar's edge runs straight and there is no gap to fill.

### Prototyping it

`working.html` grew a fixed backdrop layer and a dev-only switcher — `photo` / `night` / `gradient` —
because a browser cannot sample the desktop and translucency judged against nothing is not judged at
all. The photo is the actual wallpaper this was tuned against, vendored beside the fonts for the same
reason they are (a design file that silently falls back is one you can review and be wrong about):
mid-tone sky over high-frequency slate, which stresses light and dark chrome at once. `night` is that
same image graded down rather than a second binary. None of it ports to the app.

The prototype earned its keep and also showed its limit. It caught the compounding rule, the notch and
the sidebar step; it could not predict the material's tint, and its alphas did not survive contact with
a real desktop at native resolution — dark moved 0.88 → 0.97. A CSS blur over a downscaled JPEG is a
good model of an untinted blur and a bad model of how much of it you can afford.

*Rejected:* chrome-only and everything-but-terminal scopes (above); per-pane `backdrop-filter` in the
design file, which browsers compound and no AppKit route can reproduce, so the prototype would have
promised a look the port could not deliver; `state = .followsWindowActiveState` on the material, back
when there was a material — AppKit's default drops the effect whenever the window loses focus, and on a
shell that is translucent by design that reads as the window breaking.
## Tabs-mode branches carry their session facts without growing a third tree level

Tabs mode removes session rows from the sidebar because those sessions already live in the tab
strip. That made the branch the deepest visible row, but the old compact branch shell still spent
space on a disclosure chevron and hid the session count in a tooltip. The branch now uses that space
as a 46px two-line summary: its name first, then `N sessions · activity`.

The right edge is a fixed pair of 16px slots: pull-request state, then the branch's highest-priority
session state. Empty slots remain reserved, so names and status never jump between branches. Live,
error, needs-input, unread, pending, and PR states keep their established glyphs and colours; idle
activity moves into the facts line. Hover actions cover the whole rail rather than leaving a status
glyph behind.

This is tabs-mode presentation only. Classic sidebar mode keeps its 30px disclosure row, middle
truncation, inline PR identity, and activity rail unchanged. Rename preserves the two-line tabs-mode
geometry, and the status-only rail has explicit VoiceOver labels because it is the sole sidebar
signal once session rows are absent.

The HTML source and native SwiftUI app ship the same layout. Gate: `swift build`, an independent
code-to-spec audit, tabs-on and tabs-off screenshots from isolated running app instances, and the
`working.html` / `big-picture-design.html` subset diff.

## The pre-notarization Gatekeeper check asserts *why* it was rejected

`release.sh` assesses the freshly signed app with `spctl` before sending it to Apple. That stage's
only passing answer is `rejected` / `source=Unnotarized Developer ID` — Gatekeeper is right, the
ticket does not exist yet. The check was there to catch the *other* rejections, the ones that mean
the build is broken: unsigned nested code, a missing hardened runtime. But those are also a
`rejected`, and the line ended in `|| true`, so every outcome printed the same word and continued.
A guard that cannot fail is decoration, and the one reader it had was left to either panic at
`rejected` or learn to skim past it — which is how the real failure would have gone by.

It now captures the verdict, prints it, and requires the reason to be `source=Unnotarized Developer
ID`, dying on any other rejection. The stage also labels itself in the log, so the expected
`rejected` no longer needs a source comment to interpret it. The post-stapling assess is unchanged:
that one must be `accepted`, and `pipefail` already made it fatal.

Recorded alongside it in the release skill: `release.sh` must not be edited while it runs. Bash
reads a script incrementally, so an in-place rewrite resumes execution at a byte offset that now
falls mid-line. A release is ~20 minutes mostly spent waiting on Apple, which is precisely when
editing it feels free.
## Claude Code answers for its own colours in light mode

Light-mode Claude Code was unreadable, and the first thing worth recording is that it was unreadable
for a reason nobody would guess from looking at the theme: `TerminalTheme`'s light palette was
correct, `AgentTheme` was already syncing Claude Code's `theme` key, and the light theme it selects
is mostly sound. The failure was in *when* the key is read.

**`theme` in `~/.claude.json` is read once, at startup.** Measured directly: a session was started on
`dark`, the key rewritten to `light` underneath it, and the frame forced to redraw — it stayed dark,
four seconds later too. So the old sync only ever reached *new* sessions. Flip Synth to light with
work in progress and every open session keeps painting the dark theme onto a near-white surface,
where Claude Code's body text (`#ffffff`) measures **1.06:1** and **57 of its 72 colour tokens** fall
under 4.5:1. That is the whole of "I can't read the output", and no amount of correcting the light
theme would have touched it, because the sessions were never on the light theme.

**`theme: "auto"` is worse, and is the trap.** "Auto (match terminal)" does not match the terminal.
Claude Code enables DEC mode 2031 (`\e[?2031h`, theme-change notifications) and then ignores what
arrives: fed `CSI ? 997 ; 2 n` (light), then dark, then light again, with a full repaint after each,
it stayed on the dark theme throughout — and starts dark with no answer at all. The old `AgentTheme`
guarded on `current == "dark" || current == "light"`, so an `auto` user was deliberately *skipped* and
left in exactly the unreadable state. `auto` is now adopted, on the grounds that following the
appearance is what the setting asks for and Synth actually does it.

### The lever is the custom-theme directory, because it is the only live one

Claude Code watches `~/.claude/themes` and reloads on any change. So Synth ships a theme of its own,
`custom:synth`, and re-themes by rewriting that one file — verified to re-theme a **running** session
within a second, in both directions. Three things follow from the single `base` field, which is why
it is one file and not a light/dark pair: the 72 colour tokens, the diff renderer, and the syntax
highlighter, which Claude Code picks from the *resolved base* (GitHub for light, Monokai Extended for
dark) and not from the theme's name — checked by pointing a `base: dark` custom theme at it and
reading the picker back.

Writes are conditional on content changing. `applyTheme` runs on every window move between displays,
and each needless write wakes every running session's watcher.

### What the 20 overrides are, and what was deliberately left alone

The token names and their light values were read out of Claude Code's own theme editor, one row at a
time — they are not in the binary as text, the values live in bun bytecode. Each override keeps its
token's hue and saturation and lowers only lightness: the least change that clears the floor, so
Claude Code still looks like Claude Code. Text is held to 4.5:1 and four non-text tokens to 3:1.

The worst offender was **`subtle` at 2.06:1** — the dim grey every hint, timestamp and token count is
written in, which is what "grey text on a white background" turns out to be. Then `chromeYellow`
(1.61), `promptBorder` (2.68), `professionalBlue` (2.75), and Claude's own `#d77757` (2.96).

Three left alone, each for a reason that is the finding:
- **`diffAddedWord` / `diffRemovedWord` read like foregrounds and are not.** They are the fill behind
  a changed *word* inside a diff line, and Claude Code paints black on them. Deepening them to clear
  4.5:1 as ink drove the ink *on* them from ~17:1 down to 4.28:1. A fill is corrected by what it has
  to carry, not by its own ratio against a surface it never touches. Measured, then reverted.
- **`inverseText` is white by design** and only ever painted on a fill, so the fill has to earn the
  ratio — which is why `professionalBlue`, its usual backing, is corrected instead.
- **`clawd_background` is the mascot's silhouette.** Brand artwork, not a contrast decision.

### Two things are recorded rather than fixed, because Synth holds no lever

- **The syntax highlighter is a separate palette**, selected wholesale from the base rather than per
  token. On the light surface it leaves `#0086b3` at 3.90:1 and a faint `#333333` at 4.15:1. The
  asserted passes run with `CLAUDE_CODE_SYNTAX_HIGHLIGHT=0` so the theme's own colours are measured
  in isolation rather than blamed for it.
- **One colour belongs to no theme at all.** Forcing *every* token to magenta and re-rendering left
  `#5769f7` (4.14:1, the file path in a `Bash(...)` header) untouched. That is the proof it is
  hard-coded, and the reason the gate exempts it instead of failing on it.

Dark is ridden untouched, the same asymmetry `TerminalTheme` makes: dark is what all of these colours
were chosen against. It ships two of its own failures — the `❯` prompt marker at 1.48:1 on the
composer fill, and white at 3.09:1 on a diff word highlight — so dark is *pinned* just under what it
renders today rather than gated: not a claim, a tripwire.

### `t24_agentcontrast` measures this from the bytes, not the tokens

`t13_termcontrast` gates the sixteen palette slots a terminal owns, and Claude Code uses none of
them. The new suite replays a real session through a small terminal emulator (`ttygrid`) and reads
out every run of ink with the background it sits on, because only the settled grid is what a reader
sees — a TUI sets a foreground once and then moves the cursor around for a dozen lines, and redraws a
row three times before the frame lands. `ccontrast` resolves each cell the way ghostty would,
including faint, which is a blend rather than a colour. The override table is parsed out of
`AgentTheme.swift` rather than restated, so the gate cannot pass a theme the app does not ship.

The fixture is a transcript of our own, replayed with `--resume` — no sign-in, no token spent, and
the same diff, code fence, todo list, collapsed result and failed command on every machine. Three
bugs in the harness itself were caught by one assertion, that a line only the fixture can produce is
actually on screen: without it the suite measured a **trust prompt**, whose four colours pass
everything. The causes were all path-shaped — no `chdir`, then a `/var` vs `/private/var` mismatch,
then Claude Code's project-directory encoding, which flattens *every* non-alphanumeric to `-` and not
just the separators.

Two emulator bugs came from feeding it incrementally, and both would have been invisible in a
one-shot capture: a pty read can end mid-escape-sequence (`\e[38;2;51;51;51m` split after `\e[3`
renders as `8;2;51;51;51m` inside a word) and mid-character (box drawing arriving as replacement
characters, so it no longer classified as chrome).

`AgentTheme.sync` now takes its home directory as a parameter, and it has to: `NSHomeDirectory()`
reads the password database rather than `$HOME` for a process that is not sandboxed, so a test that
exports `HOME` and calls it will rewrite the developer's own `~/.claude.json` while believing it is
working in a temp directory. It did exactly that once.


## The archive becomes a place you can stand, and the design files catch up to `ArchiveSweeper`

`ArchiveSweeper` and its two settings rows have shipped in the native app for a while; the design
files had neither. The archive existed only as a verb — a row leaving the sidebar, a folder
reclaimed "later" — with no surface listing what is still on disk and no statement of the rule that
decides when it stops being.

**Synth tab ▸ Archived worktrees.** `SettingsPane.swift`'s switch and grace picker
(Never / 7 / 14 / 30 days, default 7), plus two new rows that have no counterpart in the app:
`Most worktrees archived` (25) and `Most disk archived` (50 GB). The app bounds how long the grace
lasts; these bound what it costs.

Only the switch carries a description, and only the half of the app's line you can't read off the
controls — that the sweep touches nothing unrecoverable and never deletes the branch. The pickers
say their own rule; a sentence under "Never · 7 · 14 · 30 days" would only say it again, slower.
Turning the switch off folds the other three rows away.

**The constraint on the budgets, and the thing to check any future condition against: a budget
brings an unblocked folder's turn forward; it never lets one through a gate.** ArchiveSweeper's
invariant is that every byte it reclaims is reconstructible from a remote, so a disk cap that
evicted the oldest folder regardless would be a hole straight through it. An over-budget archive
full of unpushed work stays put and says so.

**Project tab ▸ Archived.** The per-project list: name, age, size, and the sweeper's verdict —
`commits not pushed`, `PR still open`, `6 days left`, `checking…`, with the accent reserved for the
one row about to go. Those are `ArchiveSweeper.Block`'s own strings minus its `— kept` suffix,
which a chip sitting on a row that is plainly still there does not need; ⌘K puts it back, where the
reason is flat text with no row under it to carry that.

The section states no policy in prose. It doesn't need to: the verdicts are the policy, computed
from the same three knobs the Synth tab sets, so the list cannot describe a rule the app isn't
running. Sweep off and the verdicts simply aren't there. Under the list, two ratios and no
sentence — `8 / 25 worktrees · 7.8 / 50 GB` — because a cap you can't see coming is
indistinguishable from folders vanishing at random, and which folders it called early is already on
their own rows. With no cap set, or the sweep off, the line disappears.

Four rows, scrolling, fifth visibly cut: a project with thirty archived branches must not push the
rest of Settings out of reach, and the cut is the only honest scroll cue on a list whose scrollbar
hides until touched.

**Restore and delete.** Restore puts the branch row back — archived this session it keeps its
detached node and slots back where it stood, archived earlier its row is rebuilt. Permanent delete
confirms, and confirms in the ⌘K frame: the app has one confirm surface, so Settings' trash opens
`deleteWorktreeFrame` rather than growing a dialog of its own. "Confirms from every surface" means
every surface routes there, not that each grows its own wording to keep in step — the live-branch
`Delete worktree now` and the archived entry are now literally one frame with two callers, Cancel
preselected in both. Past the confirm it parks on the same destructive undo card as every other
delete.

Two divergences fixed while here. ⌘K's archived list was reading the global array from inside a
project frame — now project-scoped like the frame containing it. And its rows restored on ↵, making
"or delete one now" in its own placeholder unreachable; they now drill into the Restore / Delete
worktree now frame `Palette.swift` has had all along.

### The native app

Ported the same day. `FolderSize` is new — nothing in the app measured a folder before, and the
size cap needs it; it walks off the main actor into an observable cache, `node_modules` included,
because a disk-usage number that skips the biggest directory is not one. `archiveMaxCount` /
`archiveMaxGB` follow the existing pref shape. `Block.chip` derives from `Block.line` by dropping
the suffix, so the 23 strings stay written once. `Verdict` gained the display strings; the verdict
map lost its `@ObservationIgnored`, without which a pane would never redraw when a tick filled it.

The budget promotes by re-running `ArchiveSweeper.evaluate` with `graceSeconds: 0` — the same
evidence pass, not a path around it. `archivedFolder` (held folder if the sweeper has moved it,
else the worktree) is the single answer to "which bytes", because the row, the header, the footer
and the cap must not each pick their own.

Divergences fixed on the way: `archiveStatusLine` said "archived 4h" where the design and the new
Settings row both say "4h ago"; the delete confirm crammed its consequence and its way out into
one placeholder and dropped the undo clause to fit, where its own neighbour the restart confirm
already argues for the note; and the second-opinion chip was indistinguishable from a verdict.

Gate: the budget algorithm unit-tested by extracting the shipped `overBudget` out of the Swift and
compiling it — blocked-never-promoted at any age or overage, both caps, No cap, exactly-at-cap,
all-blocked — plus `FolderSize.format` proven byte-identical to `fmtSize` over seven probes. Then
`swift build` clean, and a driven instance over its control socket on a seeded repo of five real
archived worktrees: the Synth tab's four rows, the project tab's list (ages, sizes, verdict chips,
the cut fifth row, the archive-wide footer), ⌘K's project-scoped `Archived · 5` drilling into
Restore / Delete worktree now, and the confirm frame with Cancel preselected. An independent
static audit against `working.html` found the three divergences above; they are fixed.

Both design files and the native app. Design gate: the policy unit-tested against the real source
pulled out of the design file — grace, the invariant (blocked never becomes eligible however old
or over budget, and is never called early), both caps, No cap, the switch, Never, the
second-opinion rule, and the bulk brake at 5 — 18 assertions. Then archive → restore → delete →
undo driven in the page, every knob exercised against the verdicts downstream of it, per-project
scoping checked in Settings and ⌘K, light and dark, no console errors, and the `working.html` /
`big-picture-design.html` subset diff.
