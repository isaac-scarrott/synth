# 2026-08-07

## Branch hover card (tabs mode)

Tabs mode buys its 2-deep sidebar by taking the session rows out of the tree; the branch row
carries a facts line and a single roll-up glyph in their place. The gap that leaves is sharpest at
exactly the wrong moment: hovering a branch fades both rail slots to 0 to make room for the action
cluster, so the instant you reach for a worktree you lose the only per-session signal it had. A
card to the right of the row is what pays that back — the hidden rows, unpacked for as long as the
pointer is on them, and nothing more.

Scope is one question: **is anything in this worktree waiting on me or broken, and is anything
still moving?** The row already says what the branch is, so the card never repeats it. Ranked, what
earned a place: the per-session state glyph (the roll-up says *something* needs input; the card
says *which one* — this is the whole feature), the session's identity, unread, a counter where the
session actually has one, and the PR *number* the rail's bare glyph has no room for.

Every row **clones** the tree row's own `.session__icon` and `.ind` node rather than re-deriving
state from it. The card therefore introduces no status vocabulary of its own and cannot drift out
of sync with the rail it stands in for — the copper owner-mark on a Claude-owned browser, the beat
spheres, the attention glyphs all arrive for free. Rows stay in strip order, never re-sorted by
state: the card has to map onto the strip you are about to land in, and rows must not move under
the pointer. Idle sessions past seven collapse to a `+N idle` line, so the card is never something
you scroll — a card you scroll is a window.

**No clocks, and no percentages.** The meta column carries a fact only where the session genuinely
produces one — `step 4/6` from a build, `18 failed` from a test run. Everything else is silent. An
elapsed timer was the first draft and it was wrong twice over: the glyph already says whether a
session needs you, so `waiting 4m` beside a `?` adds an ageing number that never once changed
whether you'd go there, and a card that shows `done 5h ago` is only honest while it is open. A
percentage would be worse still — a promise about a future nothing here can see. The `.beat` sphere
already says "alive without promising a finish"; a bar beside it would be this design's first lie.

The branch name is not repeated anywhere in the card. The row your pointer is physically touching
is showing it, and an inch of duplication is what trains the eye to skip a surface. What the row
*can't* say goes on one line below a hairline: the PR number the rail's bare state glyph has no
room for, and the diffstat — `⑂ #482 · +412 −128 · 6 ahead of main`. That line is deliberately
monochrome; this app spends colour on state, and a diffstat is not a state.

**Read-only and `pointer-events: none`.** That one decision removes the hover-trap, the safe
triangle, the bridge element, focus management and ARIA menu semantics all at once. It also keeps
the app to one floating action surface: right-click on a row opens the pinned ⌘K frame (249/253
deleted the old `.menu` on purpose), and clicking the row lands you in the strip. When acting on a
specific session is worth a shortcut, it belongs in that ⌘K frame, not in a second popover.

Timing is the difference between this feeling native and feeling cheap. 350ms cold (100ms fires
while you scrub the tree; 700ms reads as broken), then a 400ms warm window during which the next
branch opens in 60ms, because a vertical list of branches is unusable otherwise. 120ms grace on
leaving. Row → row **repositions the one node** with a 130ms glide instead of destroying and
rebuilding it; recreate-per-row is the flicker. It dies instantly — no fade, no warm window — on
nav scroll, on pointerdown, on window blur, on resize and on Esc, because a card anchored to a row
that just moved is telling a lie. It is suppressed entirely while dragging, while a row is being
renamed, when the branch holds no sessions, and whenever tabs mode is off (with the tree 3-deep the
sessions are already on screen, and the card would just be the same rows, floating).

Anchored to the row, not the sidebar, so a resized sidebar is handled for free: `row.right + 8`,
top-aligned with a 4px lift, 300px fixed width (a card that resizes per row jitters as you run down
the list), clamped rather than flipped — the sidebar is pinned left of a window always far wider
than 260 + 300, so a horizontal flip would be dead code that only ever fires as a bug. No arrow:
the 8px gap plus the top alignment already read as attached, a clamped card makes every arrow look
broken, and nothing else in this app has one. Materially it is the `.cm-peek` / `.notif` family
verbatim — `--glass`, `saturate(180%) blur(20px)`, a 0.5px `--line`, `--notif-shadow`, radius 12 —
so dark mode needed no bespoke rule at all. In at 140ms with a 4px slide out of the row (direction
encodes origin), out at 90ms on opacity alone; reduced motion keeps the fade and drops both.

The a11y half is not the card. It is `aria-hidden` — a visual affordance, not a source of content —
and the same answer rides the branch row's own label, refreshed wherever the roll-up is:
*"feat/command-menu — 7 sessions, 1 needs input, 1 failed, 2 running, 3 unread."* Deliberately not
bound to keyboard focus: J/K walks the tree fast, and a card strobing once per row is miserable.

Landed in `working.html` and `big-picture-design.html`.

Ported to the app the same day, two isolated worktree slices merged. `BranchHoverCard.swift` is the
card, its hover view-model and the root overlay; the model is owned by `RootView` and injected
rather than living on `AppStore`, because nothing outside the row and the overlay has any business
reading it — no persistence, no automation verb, no keyboard path. The overlay mounts as a second
`.overlayPreferenceValue` with its own anchor key (not `MenuAnchorKey`, which is the kebab
cluster's), between the notification deck and the modals — the native form of z-index 90, and what
lets the card escape the sidebar's clip. Rows reuse `SessionIcon`, `Ind`, `StatusIndicator`,
`OwnedIndicator` and `TabIcon` outright, which is the native form of the mock cloning the tree row's
own nodes: the copper owner-mark on a Claude-owned browser arrived for free, and the card cannot
disagree with the rail it stands in for.

Two things the design asks for that the app could not answer, handled rather than faked. **The meta
counter has no data**: `Session` carries no numeric field and the hook wire format
(`Hooks.swift`) parses five keys, none of them a count — a real `step 4/6` needs a new signal, so
the column renders empty exactly as the mock does when the attribute is absent, and is shaped for a
`String?` to drop in. **The diffstat did not exist at all**, and every `GitService` static is a
blocking `Process` with a 10s timeout, so a hover could not ask. That became its own slice:
`DiffStat.swift`, a `FolderSizeCache`-shaped `@MainActor @Observable` cache with a 30s staleness
floor, warmed from the moment the card commits to opening so a pointer crossing four rows spawns
nothing. It counts merge-base against the working tree in one pass rather than summing
`base...HEAD` with a working-tree diff, which would double-count every line committed and then
re-edited; and it refuses to guess a base branch — no `origin/HEAD`, no local main/master means no
line, because a real number under a wrong label is worse than no number. It deliberately avoids
`GitService.defaultBase`, whose fallback is an untimed network call, which is not something to put
behind an involuntary gesture.

The shadow was wrong on first landing and is worth recording, because the mistake was in the
brief rather than the implementation: reusing `NotifCard`'s two-layer pair looked close enough
on paper but is measurably heavier than `--notif-shadow` — the ambient layer darker (.16 against
.13) and half again as wide (r18 ≈ 36px of blur against 30px), the contact layer at double its
alpha, the middle layer missing entirely. The card wore a visible halo where the design asks for
three quiet, tightly stacked ones. It is now the literal translation, theme-split like the token
(SwiftUI's shadow radius being roughly half a CSS blur). A notification is a persistent object
that has to hold the corner of a window; a hover card should barely lift.

One deliberate divergence: the mock kills the card on nav scroll because a `position: fixed` card
cannot follow a row moving under it. The anchor preference makes the card track its row exactly, so
there is nothing left to protect against and it tracks instead.

An independent audit against the spec found six things; four were real and are fixed in the same
port. Settings was a genuine hole (the pane swaps, the sidebar stays hoverable, the card floated
over it). `pointerStale` moved out of the standing suppression set and into the row's hover
callback — read from a view body it invalidated the root on every keystroke, and since raising a
suppression cuts the card, typing dismissed a card the mock leaves standing. The tabs-mode row kept
a `.help` tooltip that arrived a second after the card to repeat the branch name the card
deliberately omits. And the accessibility label sat on a `ZStack` that is not an element, so
SwiftUI pushed it onto the buttons inside.

Verified by driving the built app headless with an isolated `SYNTH_STATE_DIR`, tabs mode forced
through `NSArgumentDomain` (`-synth-tabs '<true/>'`) rather than by touching the real pref, and
screenshotted in-process in both themes. The diffstat in those captures is this branch's own,
measured live. Unverified and stated as such: the 350/60/120/130ms timings and the 4pt slide, which
no screenshot can show, and genuine pointer behaviour — a driven offscreen window takes no real
mouse, so `onContinuousHover` on true movement and the row-to-row glide were reasoned through, not
exercised.
