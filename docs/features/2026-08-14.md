## The simulator speaks the browser's language

The browser pane had grown a whole vocabulary — a framed card, a toolbar of hardware-sized
buttons, one identity field that names what is loaded and reopens the "open…" surface, a mono
badge for the number that moves while you drive, tools that light while their mode is on, a
docked panel, notices over the view, comment mode. The simulator had none of it: a device
frame floating in an empty pane. Same app, two different products.

So the vocabulary was extracted rather than copied. Everything a second surface would want is
now named `pane-*` and shared by both: `pane-surface`, `pane-bar`, `pane-btn`, `pane-omni`,
`pane-badge`, `pane-field`, `pane-rec`, `pane-drop`, `pane-notice`, `pane-dock`. Comment mode
moved out of the browser entirely into `wireComments(host, openEl, hooks)` — pins, the scope
you widen, the batch, the island, delivery, all of it — with the surface handing in only what
is its own: whether there is anything to comment on, the *place* a comment belongs to and how
to get back to it, the view's scale, and who receives a batch nobody owns.

The simulator then says the same things in hardware. Left cluster: Home, Lock, Rotate — press,
rather than back/forward/reload. Identity field: the bundle id of the running app, opening the
same floated launcher the browser's address drop is, over Installed instead of Recent, taking
a bundle id or a deep link. Badge: the device's viewport in points, which moves when you
rotate. Then comment mode, the device log (Console · Network · Views) docked exactly where
DevTools docks, and hand-off to Simulator.app. ⌘K carries a Device group beside the browser's
Page group; ⌘L, ⌘R, ⌥⌘I keep their meanings, and ⌘⇧H / ⌘⇧R are home and rotate.

Two decisions worth keeping. **The app's screen is never rebuilt to drive the device** — home,
lock and rotate leave it standing (hidden, or re-laid-out from the frame's own custom
properties), so a comment left on the app is still on it, still anchored to its element, when
you come back; only a launch replaces it, because a launch really does. And **a simulator is
never adopted**: a browser re-nests under the agent its comments spawn, but a simulator's row
carries the booted device's own liveness, and ownership would take that indicator's slot — the
batch still spawns the agent that receives it.
