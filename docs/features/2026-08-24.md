## A click outside the scratch terminal is Esc, and nothing more

⌘⇧T puts a shell over a dimmed app, and until now the dim was inert: the only ways out were
⌘⇧T, ⌘W, Esc and `exit`. Every other overlay in Synth — ⌘K, every dialog, the shortcuts sheet —
closes when you click off it, so the one surface that didn't read as stuck. The original reason
was real (a stray click on the dim must never kill a running command) but it was an argument for
a *rule*, not for having no click at all.

**The click is Esc's other hand.** It resolves on exactly what Esc resolves on — an idle prompt
(and, in the design, an empty line) — so there is still only one rule to know and the amber dot
beside the branch name is still the only tell for which meaning is live. With a job in the
foreground the click is nothing at all: not a close, and deliberately not the busy-close confirm
either, because a stray click putting a dialog over a job you were only watching is the same
papercut in a politer coat. ⌘⇧T and ⌘W keep the confirm — they are chords you meant.

`dismissScratchTerminalFromOutside()` is where the rule lives, so the tap gesture on the scrim and
the automation seam can't drift from each other; `scrDismissable()` does the same job in
working.html, shared by the Esc branch and the dim's `mousedown`. `t12_scratch` gained the two
checks that matter (7b: a click at an idle prompt closes; 12b: a click while busy leaves the job
alone and raises no confirm), and the wiring the harness can't see — a driven window ignores the
mouse — was proved against a real build with a real click at a real coordinate.
