# 2026-09-04

## 0.40.0 ships — Inspect is a session

The release carries the inspect work from 2026-09-01 and nothing else: DevTools stops being a
panel the browser docks under itself and becomes a session at the browser's own level — a row, a
resizable pane, a peer tab, a thing ⌘W closes — opened by right-click → Inspect, ⌥⌘I, or the
bar's `</>`, one per browser, split beneath the page. The native pane hosts the real Chromium
inspector rather than the mock's skeleton, driving the page off the instance's own CDP endpoint,
and the inspect belongs to its browser the way a browser belongs to its Claude, cascading closed
with it.

`CFBundleVersion` 663, tag `v0.40.0`. dmg and zip both notarized and stapled, verified
credential-less with quarantine set: the image is `accepted / Notarized Developer ID`, and so is
the app *inside* it on its own — stapler valid there and on the extracted zip, which is what keeps
a dragged-out copy working when Apple is unreachable. Appcast newest 0.40.0 at 663,
`sparkle:edSignature` on all 18 enclosures, deltas from 637–654 at 1.0–1.3 MB against a 164 MB
full download. The shipped bundle's `CHANGELOG.json` leads with 0.40.0, so Synth → Changelog
names this build.

No `synth-site` push: `landing/` has not changed since 0.37.0, and the Download buttons point at
the bucket's stable `Synth.dmg` alias, which now resolves to this build.

## The site is rebuilt in `site/`, and it argues one thing

`landing/` (mirrored to `synth-site`) was a feature tour: speed, command menu, branches, browser,
notifications — five equal beats, in the order they happened to be built. Attention sat fifth. A
reader came away knowing Synth has panes, which is the one thing every competitor also has.

The rebuild is `site/index.html`, written from scratch against a single claim: **Synth spends your
attention as carefully as it can, and gives every piece of work its own place to happen.** Four
beats, each earning its place rather than reporting a feature:

1. **Somewhere to put the work** — worktrees as the precondition (no isolation, no per-thing state,
   nothing to indicate), then *handoff* as the payload: work that doesn't belong here asks for its
   own branch and wakes an agent already briefed. Auto-archive closes it, so the tree stays the size
   of what is actually being worked on. Handoff is the most demonstrable thing in the app and the
   old page never mentioned it at all.
2. **Being told, rather than checking** — promoted from fifth to second, and widened from "you get a
   banner" to the whole grammar: two glyphs mean a person is needed, everything else is a quiet dot,
   collapsed rows roll up. The three supporting cells are deliberately mundane decisions (deck
   ordering by cost-to-miss, comment batching, switching an agent off) because the claim is credible
   only if the restraint shows up where nobody would have noticed.
3. **Hands, and the same screen** — browser and simulator merged into one section. Two sections
   diluted what is a single claim, and the element-anchored comment is the part that closes the loop.
4. **Quick** — reframed as the enabler for (2) rather than a spec: a tree you glance at only works
   if glancing is free. ⌘K demoted from its own section to evidence here; it is how you drive the
   thing, not why you would want it.

**Design: zeroleaks.ai's structure, Synth's palette.** The reference was picked for its lattice —
near-black ground, hairline rules bounding a fixed content column, eyebrow pills, bordered feature
cells, corner ticks. It already sets in Geist, so the face needed no translation. What changed is
everything chromatic: the ground is `--canvas #0d0f13` rather than pure black (the app icon's own
gradient, one step past its bottom stop), champagne `#eee0cd` is the only accent and is spent only
on the primary button, the eyebrow dot and one inline highlight.

**The status hues are the app's, meaning what they mean in the app.** `--input` blue is `?`, red is
`!`, the violet sphere is liveness, green is a running process. The page's palette and the product's
indicator grammar are one system, so the legend in section 2 is not an illustration of the app — it
is the app's own vocabulary, rendered.

**The figures are drawn, not screenshotted.** Sidebar rows, the notification deck, the handoff
approval and the browser comment island are HTML and CSS built from the same tokens, which is why
they cannot drift from a build the way the six stale PNGs in `landing/img/` had. It also means the
page needs no new screenshots to ship, and any it later gains are additive.

*Verified in-browser at 1440 and 390:* zero horizontal overflow at both (the hero glow's `-6%`
right bleed was pushing 23px past the viewport and was cut to `0`), Geist loading from the
self-hosted variable TTFs, reveal animations bypassed under `prefers-reduced-motion`. The
requirements line is checked rather than assumed — `Package.swift` declares `.macOS(.v14)` and
`dist.sh` runs a bare `swift build -c release`, so the DMG is arm64 only.

*Not done:* `landing/` is left in place and still what `synth-site` serves; switching the published
site over to `site/` is a separate call. No domain is attached to either — Pages reports
`cname: null`.
