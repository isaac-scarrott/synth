# 2026-09-14

## The browser flickered on every click — the theme nudge was resizing the page to a pixel

Clicking in a page still flashed the browser white after the 0.42.1 pane-entrance fix. Recorded
on the running app with a 60fps window capture while Isaac clicked around the storefront harness:
each click that changed the address produced three whole-page white frames, at +0ms, +300ms and
+900ms. That is the retry schedule of `kickPageTheme` — the four-attempt colour-scheme repaint
nudge from 2026-09-06, which `applyPageTheme()` runs on every `addressDidChange`.

The nudge was `Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride` at 1×1 followed by `clearDeviceMetricsOverride`.
That is a real resize to one pixel and back, and Chromium paints its background between the two
frames whenever they don't land in the same vsync — which on a page busy laying itself out after a
navigation is most of the time. Sent alone over CDP to the same page, the 1×1 pair blanked the
page for a frame in 2 of 2, then 1 of 3, then 1 of 4 rounds; a click synthesised inside the
renderer (`Input.dispatchMouseEvent`) never did, which is what put the AppKit and CEF sides in the
clear and pointed at the CDP traffic Synth itself sends after a click.

The fix keeps the nudge and takes the resize out of it: `width: 0, height: 0` is CDP's "no size
override", so device emulation is still enabled and cleared — the transition that forces the
layout pass — but the viewport never changes. Measured the same way against the same page: 0 blank
frames in 8 rounds, interleaved with the 1×1 control that still blanked. A same-size override
(`1343×980`) behaved identically, but needs the viewport size and 0×0 doesn't.

Not re-verified: that the 0×0 form still un-sticks the stalled colour-scheme repaint the nudge was
introduced for. That state didn't reproduce during this session, so the claim rests on Chromium
enabling device emulation being the same layout-forcing transition regardless of size. If a page
is seen holding a stale theme after this, that is the thing to test first.

Also ruled out on the way: the 2026-09-08 entrance fix holds (a tab switch is one frame), and the
window's first-responder follower does nothing in a single pane. The frame captures live in the
session's scratch, not the repo.

## 0.43.2 ships — the browser stops flashing on click

A patch release carrying one app change: the colour-scheme repaint nudge no longer resizes the
page to a pixel and back, so a navigation no longer costs up to four whole-page white frames.
That is the whole difference from 0.43.1; nothing else moved.

## Archived means cleaned up — the worktree sweeper drops its gate chain

The archive clean-up had grown into twenty-four reasons to keep a folder: uncommitted edits,
untracked files, unpushed commits, a half-finished rebase, a lock, a nested repo, a precious
`.env`, dirty submodules, an open PR, a closed PR, no PR, GitHub unreachable, a process whose cwd
was inside, another Synth instance, a symlink — each with its own chip on the Archived row and its
own "— kept" line in ⌘K, plus a second-opinion rule that wanted two clean readings a day apart, a
bulk brake that raised a Review card past five eligible folders, per-tick and per-launch caps, a
dry-run that was on by default on the dev channel, and a hold that renamed the folder aside for
two weeks before a separate reaper deleted it. Isaac's verdict: "the rules about if it's clean or
not is stupid. If it's been archived, then just clean it up." And: interrupt as little as
possible — a card when worktrees were cleaned up, a card when something went wrong, nothing else.

The rule now fits in a sentence. A merged branch is archived for you; an archived worktree's
folder is deleted after the wait (1 / 7 / 14 / 30 days, default 7), or sooner while the archive is
over its count or disk cap, oldest first; a restore after that re-cuts the checkout from the
branch; and a merged branch the remote has already dropped ends with its ref, and its row goes
with it. Nothing inside the folder is consulted. `ArchiveSweeper` is 100 lines: `due()` is a date
comparison plus the cap loop, `countdown()` is what the row says ("6 days left" / "next
clean-up"), and `branchEnd()` is unchanged. Eleven git probes, the `lsof` pass, the foreign-
instance check, the hold/release/reap trio, `lastCleanSweepEval` on the model and in persistence,
the verdict cache, and the Review card all go. The fast delete path `deleteWorktreeFolder` already
does what the hold and reaper did between them, in one git chain.

"Merged" for the finished-row pass is the branch being an ancestor of the default branch, or
GitHub reporting the PR merged (a squash leaves the branch an ancestor of nothing). A merged
branch with a commit past the merge is not merged and stays in the tree; archived by hand, its
folder still goes, and the commit is on the ref — the gate archives `not-pushed`, watches the
folder vanish, checks `git log` still has the commit, restores, and finds `local.txt` in the
re-cut checkout. That is the whole safety story now: a git ref is the durable thing, a folder is
a cache of it, and the ref is only ever deleted once the remote has it too.

Settings → Archived worktrees is a switch and three four-option pickers: `Delete after`, `Most
worktrees archived`, `Most disk archived`. "Never" is gone from the wait — the switch is the off —
and a stored 0 reads as the default. `Delete the branch too` folded into the switch: retiring a
merged, remote-dropped ref is part of cleaning up, not a second policy. The Archived list's row
reads "4h ago · 1.2 GB · 6 days left", or "4h ago · cleaned up" once the folder has gone and the
row is waiting on its ref; ⌘K's line is "archived 4h ago · 6 days left". The archive-wide ratio
under the list stays, because a cap you can't see coming is folders vanishing at random.

Cards: one. "Cleaned up N archived worktrees" (ambient, self-dismissing) when a tick deleted
something, and the existing "Couldn't delete worktree" error with Retry if it couldn't. Archiving
a merged row and retiring its ref are silent — neither touches disk, and both are a ⌘K away. The
once-per-launch card for stray folders git doesn't know stays, since those are the one thing the
clean-up can't account for.

`t9_archive` is rewritten around the new rule and passes 47 checks: merged rows archive in one
tick and keep their folders until the next; a dirty and an untracked worktree lose theirs; the
remote-dropped ref retires and the remote-listed one doesn't; the undo window still changes
nothing; the count cap takes the older of two archived folders inside their wait and leaves the
newer counting "12 days left". `t10_toasts` loses its bulk-brake checks and gains one: the tick
that cleaned up raised exactly one card. `sandbox.sh` and `docs/archive-sandbox.md` describe the
eight remaining scenarios. `SYNTH_ARCHIVE_EVAL_GAP_SECONDS`, `SYNTH_ARCHIVE_HOLD_SECONDS`,
`SYNTH_ARCHIVE_BULK_BRAKE` and `-synth-archive-dry-run` no longer exist.

Supersedes the 2026-08-20 finished-row gate chain and the hold/reaper design behind the
0.14.0 archive.

## 0.44.0 ships — archived worktrees clean themselves up

A minor release carrying one app change: the worktree sweeper's gate chain is gone. A merged
branch is archived for you; an archived folder is deleted after the wait you pick (1 / 7 / 14 / 30
days) or sooner over the count or disk cap, oldest first; a restore re-cuts the checkout from the
branch; a merged branch the remote has dropped retires with its row. Settings → Archived worktrees
is one switch and three pickers, and the only card is "Cleaned up N archived worktrees" plus the
delete error. It is a minor rather than a patch because what the switch does and what the
Settings pane offers both changed under the user's feet.
