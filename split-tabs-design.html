Split tabs

Today a split's members leave the tab language entirely: they shrink to 22px chips, drop the indicator slot, drop the active bar, drop the hover wash and stop shrinking to fit. Below, the members go back to being tabs — every feature intact — and grouping is carried by one added mark. Four candidates for that mark.

Two selection states. With a member selected, that pane owns the surface and the tab takes the raised fill + focus bar, exactly as a lone tab does. With none selected — a lone tab full-screened over the split, which the layout keeps stashed underneath — every member is inert and the group mark is the only thing still saying "there's a split waiting here". The toggle above flips every strip on this page between the two.

Today
  • No status. A member that needs input or errors shows nothing — the one signal the strip exists to carry.
  • Second visual language. 22px pill vs 30px tab; the eye reads two kinds of thing in one strip.
  • No hover, no active bar — the ring does double duty for active, and hover is silent.
  • Fixed width. flex: 0 0 auto, so a wide split pushes the + off the strip instead of ellipsizing.
  • Cramped × at 5px right padding, and the unread dot's halo is drawn for the strip, not the chip.
  • The + can still be clipped. The strip promises the + always stays visible, but a tab can't shrink past its icon + indicator + × (~70px), so a crowded strip clips it — a group's extra chips make that arrive sooner. Visible in the narrower cells below.

Candidates

All four share the same fix: a member is a full tab again (30px, icon, name, indicator, ×, active bar, hover, ellipsis), the seam between members lightens to an inset hairline, and the seam that ends the group stays a normal strip seam. Only the group mark differs.

A · RailRecommended

A 2px accent rail along the group's top edge — the focus bar's grammar, moved to the opposite edge so it never collides with the active tab's own bar. Reads instantly at a glance, survives a 3-member split, and the orientation glyph tells you the split is side-by-side before you look down.

B · Bracket

A hairline accent outline around the group, open at the bottom so the active member's focus bar still runs clean to the strip edge. Strongest containment of the four; the rounded top corners cost a little of the strip's flatness.

C · Field

No container, no rail — the members simply share a faint accent field, with the orientation glyph leading. Adjacency alone carries the grouping, the way the sidebar's split band does it. Quietest; also the easiest to miss on a busy strip.

D · Position badge

Members stay ordinary tabs. The only mark is an 8px mini-map on each icon showing which half of the split that tab drives — grouping is inferred from two tabs carrying the same map. Most information per pixel (you learn which pane, not just grouped), least glanceable grouping. Pairs well with A.

Rail + badge, stacked

Both marks together: the rail says grouped, the badge says which half.

Selection states, side by side

The decision the "none selected" case forces: does the group mark hold at full strength, or rest back while nothing in it is selected? Held reads as structure — the split is a fact of the layout whether or not you're looking at it. Rested reads as focus — the strip's accent tracks where you are. Stacked split — the glyph flips to the column orientation
Group leads the strip — the rail's left end meets the sidebar's rounded corner

What the code change is

  • Members render through the same makeTabEl path with no size override — drop the 22px height, the chip radius/fill and the .tab__ind { display:none }.
  • Hover moves from .tabstrip__tabs > .tab:hover to .tab:hover, so a member washes like any other tab.
  • Members go back to flex: 0 1 auto — a wide split ellipsizes instead of shoving the + off the strip.
  • The group wrapper keeps only the mark: rail / bracket / field, plus the orientation glyph.
  • The unread halo and the .tab-bleed first-child rules need to see through a group wrapper (.tabstrip__tabs > :first-child is a group now, not a tab).

Behaviour still open

  • Does dragging the glyph move the whole group, or is it click-to-focus-the-split only?
  • Does reordering a member inside its group swap the two panes on screen?
  • ⌘W on the last member — close the tab and collapse the split, or refuse?
  • Does a group ever collapse to one tab with a member count, past ~3 members on a narrow window?
